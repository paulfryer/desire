# Literature Review: Wanting vs. Habit in Artificial Agents

**Date:** 2026-10-06

**Scope.** This review covers work relevant to testing "wanting" (need-sensitive valuation of a represented outcome) against "habit" (cached stimulus-response or cached value) in artificial agents. It uses three assays from animal learning: T1, specific-satiety outcome devaluation tested in extinction; T2, zero-shot revaluation of a never-needed nutrient (Berridge-style); and T3, the effect of training amount on devaluation sensitivity. The assays are run across a factorial of agent ingredients: interoception, need variation during training, model-free vs. meta-RL vs. learned-reward model-based vs. homeostatic planner vs. active inference. Ground-truth control agents check that each assay sorts known types correctly. The review covers foundational classics and work from 2018 to 2026. Every citation was checked against a publisher page, PubMed Central, arXiv, a proceedings site or Crossref metadata. Items that could only be partly verified are flagged. Tags: **[supports]** our design or premise, **[constrains]** it (a pitfall or condition), **[pre-empts]** part of our novelty.

## 1. Goal-directed vs. habitual control and outcome devaluation

### 1a. Animal-learning foundations

- **Adams, C. D., & Dickinson, A. (1981).** Instrumental responding following reinforcer devaluation. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology B*, 33(2b), 109–121. https://doi.org/10.1080/14640748108400816. Devaluing the earned outcome (a conditioned taste aversion, not satiety) reduced lever pressing in an extinction test, compared with devaluing a non-contingent food. **[supports]** This is the source of the "test in extinction, compare against a control outcome" logic behind T1.
- **Adams, C. D. (1982).** Variations in the sensitivity of instrumental responding to reinforcer devaluation. *QJEP B*, 34(2b), 77–98. https://doi.org/10.1080/14640748208400878. Responding after brief training was sensitive to devaluation; responding after extended training was not. **[supports]** This is the canonical result behind T3.
- **Dickinson, A. (1985).** Actions and habits: the development of behavioural autonomy. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*, 308(1135), 67–78. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1985.0010. Defines goal-directed action (sensitive to outcome value) versus habit (autonomous of it). **[supports]**
- **Dickinson, A., Nicholas, D. J., & Adams, C. D. (1983).** The effect of the instrumental training contingency on susceptibility to reinforcer devaluation. *QJEP B*, 35, 35–51. Interval schedules support habits more readily than ratio schedules. **[constrains]** The schedule, i.e. how strongly action rate is correlated with reward rate, matters as much as the amount of training.
- **Colwill, R. M., & Rescorla, R. A. (1985a).** Postconditioning devaluation of a reinforcer affects instrumental responding. *JEP: Animal Behavior Processes*, 11(1), 120–132. https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.11.1.120. This is the two-response/two-outcome design with within-subject, outcome-specific devaluation. **(1985b)** Instrumental responding remains sensitive to reinforcer devaluation after extensive training. *JEP:ABP*, 11(4), 520–536. https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.11.4.520. **[constrains]** With two actions and two outcomes, overtraining often does not produce habits.
- **Kosaki, Y., & Dickinson, A. (2010).** Choice and contingency in the development of behavioral autonomy during instrumental conditioning. *JEP:ABP*, 36(3), 334–342. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016887. With extensive training, a concurrent choice between two responses earning different outcomes kept behaviour goal-directed. A single response trained alongside non-contingent delivery of the other outcome became habitual. **[constrains]** This bears directly on how T3 should be built (see §9).
- **Dickinson, A., Balleine, B., Watt, A., Gonzalez, F., & Boakes, R. A. (1995).** Motivational control after extended instrumental training. *Animal Learning & Behavior*, 23(2), 197–206. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03199935. A classic study of extended training and motivational shifts. **[supports]** T3.
- **Balleine, B. W., & Dickinson, A. (1998).** Goal-directed instrumental action: contingency and incentive learning and their cortical substrates. *Neuropharmacology*, 37(4–5), 407–419. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0028-3908(98)00033-1. Gives the two criteria for goal-directed action: an action–outcome contingency ("belief") and an outcome incentive value acquired through incentive learning ("desire"). **[supports]** This is our working definition of wanting. **[constrains]** For instrumental action, the "desire" component is learned (see §2).
- **de Wit, S., Kindt, M., Knot, S. L., et al. (2018).** Shifting the balance between goals and habits: Five failures in experimental habit induction. *J. Exp. Psychol.: General*, 147(7), 1043–1065. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000402. Five attempts to induce habits in humans through overtraining failed. **[constrains]** A null overtraining effect is a common empirical outcome, not necessarily a broken assay. The single positive human study it set out to replicate is **Tricomi, Balleine & O'Doherty (2009)**, *Eur. J. Neurosci.* 29(11), 2225–2232.
- **Vázquez-Millán, A., Martínez-López, P., et al. (2026).** The evaluation of devaluation: Deficient outcome devaluation leads to wrongly considering goal-directed actions as habits. *Behavior Research Methods*, 58(8). https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-026-03099-6. Apparent habits came mostly from participants for whom devaluation had not worked. A group-level manipulation check does not show that devaluation worked for each individual. **[constrains]** Our assays need a manipulation check for each seed.
- **Moore, S., Wang, Z., Zhu, Z., et al. (2026).** Revealing abrupt transitions from goal-directed to habitual behavior. *Nature Communications*, 17, 4751. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71048-0. In mice, the shift to habit came abruptly after thousands of trials, and lesions of the dorsolateral striatum blocked it. **[constrains]** T3 should sample training amount densely and test for a step change, not assume a gradual slope.

### 1b. Computational models of devaluation and arbitration

- **Daw, N. D., Niv, Y., & Dayan, P. (2005).** Uncertainty-based competition between prefrontal and dorsolateral striatal systems for behavioral control. *Nature Neuroscience*, 8(12), 1704–1711. https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1560. Habits are a model-free cache and goal-directed control is a model-based tree search. The two are arbitrated by uncertainty, which accounts for both devaluation sensitivity and overtraining. **[supports]** This is the theory behind mapping PPO to habit and planners to goal-directed control.
- **Keramati, M., Dezfouli, A., & Piray, P. (2011).** Speed/accuracy trade-off between the habitual and the goal-directed processes. *PLoS Comput. Biol.*, 7(5), e1002055. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002055. **Keramati, Smittenaar, Dolan & Dayan (2016)**, *PNAS* 113(45), 12868–12873 (https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1609094113), adds "plan-until-habit": planning to a limited depth and then using cached values. **[constrains]** Planners in our factorial should report their planning depth, because a shallow planner backed by a cached value is part habit.
- **Dezfouli, A., & Balleine, B. W. (2012).** Habits, action sequences and reinforcement learning. *Eur. J. Neurosci.*, 35(7), 1036–1051. Followed by Dezfouli & Balleine (2013), *PLoS Comput. Biol.* (hierarchical action control), and Dezfouli, Lingawi & Balleine (2014), *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*, 369, 20130482. On this account habits are chunked action sequences, not model-free values. **[constrains]** "Habit" has more than one computational form.
- **Miller, K. J., Shenhav, A., & Ludvig, E. A. (2019).** Habits without values. *Psychological Review*, 126(2), 292–311. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000120. Habits are modelled as value-free tendencies to repeat past actions, in contrast to model-free RL. **[constrains]** Our fixed-habit control agent is a value-free habit in this sense. PPO is a value-based cache, which is a different kind of habit. The write-up should keep the two apart.
- **Perez, O. D., & Dickinson, A. (2020).** A theory of actions and habits: The interaction of rate correlation and contiguity systems in free-operant behavior. *Psychological Review*, 127(6), 945–971. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000201. A formal model of schedule and training effects. **[supports]** It can help pick the training schedule for T3.
- **Mannella, F., Mirolli, M., & Baldassarre, G. (2016).** Goal-directed behavior and instrumental devaluation: A neural system-level computational model. *Front. Behav. Neurosci.*, 10, 181. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2016.00181. A circuit-level model of devaluation in which outcome value depends on internal state. **[supports]**
- **Daw, N. D., Gershman, S. J., Seymour, B., Dayan, P., & Dolan, R. J. (2011).** Model-based influences on humans' choices and striatal prediction errors. *Neuron*, 69(6), 1204–1215. Introduces the two-step task. **Akam, T., Costa, R., & Dayan, P. (2015).** Simple plans or sophisticated habits? *PLoS Comput. Biol.*, 11(12), e1004648. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004648. Shows that model-free strategies can look model-based. **[constrains]** This is the main reason to rely on devaluation in extinction rather than signatures based on trial-to-trial transitions.
- **Momennejad, I., Russek, E. M., Cheong, J. H., Botvinick, M. M., Daw, N. D., & Gershman, S. J. (2017).** The successor representation in human reinforcement learning. *Nature Human Behaviour*, 1(9), 680–692. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-017-0180-8. **Russek et al. (2017)**, *PLoS Comput. Biol.* 13(9), e1005768. A successor-representation agent adjusts quickly to reward revaluation but not to transition revaluation. **[constrains]** An agent passing T1/T2 shows only that its reward is factored apart from its dynamics, not that it does full model-based planning.

### 1c. Devaluation and revaluation tests in deep or artificial agents

- **Han, D., Doya, K., Li, D., & Tani, J. (2024).** Synergizing habits and goals with variational Bayes. *Nature Communications*, 15, 4461. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48577-7. A deep variational agent in which habit is a prior over intentions and goal-directed behaviour is the goal-conditioned posterior. In a simulated T-maze it shows habit formation with training, devaluation-insensitivity after extensive training, and zero-shot goal-directed behaviour. **[pre-empts, partly]** This is the closest existing deep-agent demonstration of T1 and T3. It uses one architecture, not a factorial, and no motivational (interoceptive) manipulation.
- **van Seijen, H., Nekoei, H., Racah, E., & Chandar, S. (2020).** The LoCA regret: A consistent metric to evaluate model-based behavior in reinforcement learning. *NeurIPS 2020*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03158. **Wan, Y., Rahimi-Kalahroudi, A., Rajendran, J., Momennejad, I., Chandar, S., & van Seijen, H. (2022).** Towards evaluating adaptivity of model-based reinforcement learning methods. *ICML 2022*, PMLR 162:22536–22561. A revaluation-style metric for deep model-based RL. PlaNet and DreamerV2 adapted poorly to local reward changes. **[pre-empts, partly; constrains]** Revaluation tests of deep model-based RL already exist, but they change rewards in the environment rather than the agent's internal state. Our learned-scalar-reward model-based agent may well fail for the reasons they describe.
- **Simmons-Edler, R., Badman, R. P., Berg, F. B., et al. (2025).** Deep RL needs deep behavior analysis: Exploring implicit planning by model-free agents in open-ended environments. *NeurIPS 2025*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06981. Model-free recurrent agents in a foraging world develop planning-like behaviour. **[supports]** It argues for ethology-style assays. **[constrains]** Recurrent model-free agents may pass goal-directed tests.
- **Binz, M., & Schulz, E. (2023).** Using cognitive psychology to understand GPT-3. *PNAS*, 120(6), e2218523120. GPT-3 showed model-based signatures in the two-step task. **Momennejad, I., Hasanbeig, H., Vieira, F., et al. (2023).** Evaluating cognitive maps and planning in large language models with CogEval. *NeurIPS 2023*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15129. Includes reward-revaluation and transition-revaluation conditions. **[supports]** Animal and human revaluation paradigms are already being applied to LLMs, but without motivational-state manipulations or ground-truth calibration.

## 2. Zero-shot revaluation and state-dependent incentive salience

- **Robinson, M. J. F., & Berridge, K. C. (2013).** Instant transformation of learned repulsion into motivational "wanting". *Current Biology*, 23(4), 282–289. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.01.016. Rats learned to avoid a lever cue (a Pavlovian conditioned stimulus) that predicted intensely salty Dead Sea solution. When they first met the cue in a novel sodium-depleted state, before tasting salt in that state, they immediately approached and nibbled it. **[supports]** This is the template for T2. Note that the response is **Pavlovian**.
- **Krieckhaus, E. E., & Wolf, G. (1968).** Acquisition of sodium by rats: Interaction of innate mechanisms and latent learning. *J. Comp. Physiol. Psychol.*, 65(2), 197–201. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025547. The **instrumental** counterpart: sodium-depleted rats immediately worked more on a lever that had earlier delivered salt water. **[supports]** T2 for instrumental responses, but specifically for sodium, which may have dedicated innate circuitry.
- **Balleine, B. (1992).** Instrumental performance following a shift in primary motivation depends on incentive learning. *JEP:ABP*, 18(3), 236–250. https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.18.3.236. **Balleine & Dickinson (1998)**, *Animal Learning & Behavior*, 26(1), 46–59 (https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03199161). **Roy, D. J., Burton, T. J., & Balleine, B. W. (2026).** The motivational control of instrumental performance by nutrient-specific appetites depends on incentive learning. *bioRxiv*. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.14.732213. Together these show that instrumental performance does **not** track a new motivational state unless the animal has first experienced the outcome in that state. This held for protein and carbohydrate appetites too, and required exposure across multiple outcomes and states. **[constrains, strongly]** In animals, instant revaluation of *instrumental* responses is the exception, not the rule. An agent that fails T2 can still be goal-directed in Dickinson's sense.
- **Zhang, J., Berridge, K. C., Tindell, A. J., Smith, K. S., & Aldridge, J. W. (2009).** A neural computational model of incentive salience. *PLoS Comput. Biol.*, 5(7), e1000437. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000437. Cue-triggered "wanting" is computed at the moment of re-encounter as learned value modulated by a physiological-state factor (κ). Fits salt appetite and drug sensitization. **[supports]** Our homeostatic planner is a model-based version of κ.
- **Dayan, P., & Berridge, K. C. (2014).** Model-based and model-free Pavlovian reward learning: Revaluation, revision, and revelation. *Cogn. Affect. Behav. Neurosci.*, 14(2), 473–492. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13415-014-0277-8. Argues that Pavlovian values can be model-based, with internal state entering the evaluation, and that this explains instant revaluation. **[supports]** T2 is a model-based test.
- **Berridge, K. C. (2023).** Separating desire from prediction of outcome value. *Trends Cogn. Sci.*, 27(10), 932–946. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.07.007. Argues that "wanting" can come apart from predicted value. **[supports]** This is the conceptual target: an agent can predict an outcome without wanting it.
- **Dayan, P., & Balleine, B. W. (2002).** Reward, motivation, and reinforcement learning. *Neuron*, 36(2), 285–298. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(02)00963-7. **Niv, Y., Joel, D., & Dayan, P. (2006).** A normative perspective on motivation. *Trends Cogn. Sci.*, 10(8), 375–381. Habitual control is correct only in the motivational states seen during training. In novel states, drive can **energize** habitual actions without regard to the outcome. **[constrains]** A habit agent may change its overall response rate when needs shift. T1/T2 indices must therefore be outcome-specific, not raw rates.
- **Millidge, B., Song, Y., Lak, A., Walton, M. E., & Bogacz, R. (2024).** Reward Bases: A simple mechanism for adaptive acquisition of multiple reward types. *PLoS Comput. Biol.*, 20(11), e1012580. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012580 (preprint 2022: "Reward bases: Instantaneous reward revaluation with temporal difference learning"). Separate TD values are learned for each reward dimension and weighted by current need, which gives instant revaluation. **[pre-empts, partly]** This is a mechanism for need-weighted instant revaluation. It works only within the span of reward bases fixed in advance, so it predicts **failure for a never-needed nutrient that has no basis**. That boundary is exactly what T2 probes.
- **Uchida, Y., Hikida, T., & Yamashita, Y. (2022).** Computational mechanisms of osmoregulation: A reinforcement learning model for sodium appetite. *Front. Neurosci.*, 16, 857009. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.857009. A homeostatic-RL model of sodium appetite; the authors note that preference switching in their model requires learning. **Duriez, A., Bergerot, C., Cone, J. J., Roitman, M. F., & Gutkin, B. (2023).** Homeostatic reinforcement theory accounts for sodium appetitive state- and taste-dependent dopamine responding. *Nutrients*, 15(4), 1015. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15041015. **[supports]** Homeostatic RL is the standard model for salt appetite. Neither paper tests deep agents.
- **Byrnes, S. (2024).** Incentive learning vs Dead Sea salt experiment. *LessWrong* blog post, 25 June 2024 **[non-peer-reviewed]**. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YQ4rSTHpHeFcAmhvi. Explicitly reconciles Robinson & Berridge with Dickinson & Balleine and draws alignment implications. **[supports]** It frames the same Pavlovian-vs-instrumental tension that T2 has to address.

## 3. Homeostatic reinforcement learning and interoception in agents

- **Keramati, M., & Gutkin, B. (2011).** A reinforcement learning theory for homeostatic regulation. *NeurIPS 24*, 82–90. **Keramati & Gutkin (2014).** Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability. *eLife*, 3, e04811. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04811. Reward is defined as drive reduction, and maximizing reward is proved equivalent to maintaining homeostasis. **[supports]** This is the basis of our homeostatic planner. The papers do not separate model-free and model-based routes under a change of internal state; our factorial supplies that comparison.
- **Juechems, K., & Summerfield, C. (2019).** Where does value come from? *Trends Cogn. Sci.*, 23(10), 836–850. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.07.012. Argues that value should come from internal (homeostatic) state rather than an external scalar reward. **[supports]**
- **Yoshida, N., Arikawa, E., Kanazawa, H., & Kuniyoshi, Y. (2024).** Modeling long-term nutritional behaviors using deep homeostatic reinforcement learning. *PNAS Nexus*, 3(12), pgae540. https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae540. Deep RL agents with interoceptive input reproduce animal patterns of nutrient balancing. **Yoshida, N., Daikoku, T., Nagai, Y., & Kuniyoshi, Y. (2024).** Emergence of integrated behaviors through direct optimization for homeostasis. *Neural Networks*, 177, 106379. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106379. Agents switch behaviour according to manipulated interoception. **[supports]** Deep homeostatic RL with interoception works. Neither paper runs devaluation or never-needed-nutrient tests.
- **Yoshida, N., Sprekeler, H., & Gutkin, B. (2025).** Linking homeostasis to reinforcement learning: Internal state control of motivated behavior. *Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences*, 66, 101611. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2025.101611 (arXiv 2507.04998). A review of homeostatically regulated RL (HRRL): risk aversion, anticipatory regulation, links to deep RL. **[supports]**
- **Yoshida, N. (2025).** Meta-reinforcement learning in homeostatic regulation. *CCN 2025 abstract*. https://2025.ccneuro.org/abstract_pdf/Yoshida_2025_Meta-Reinforcement_Learning_Homeostatic_Regulation.pdf. Recurrent policies with multimodal input that are trained on internally defined reward acquire meta-RL-like exploration. **[pre-empts, partly]** This is close to our recurrent-PPO-with-interoception cell. It does not run devaluation or revaluation assays (abstract-level verification only).
- **Dulberg, Z., Dubey, R., Berwian, I. M., & Cohen, J. D. (2023).** Having multiple selves helps learning agents explore and adapt in complex changing worlds. *PNAS*, 120(28), e2221180120. Modular agents (one module per need) outperform monolithic agents as needs multiply and change. **[supports]** Need-factored value is one route to T2-like generalization, similar to reward bases.
- **Man, K., & Damasio, A. (2019).** Homeostasis and soft robotics in the design of feeling machines. *Nature Machine Intelligence*, 1(10), 446–452. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0103-7. **[supports]** Programmatic case for interoception and homeostasis as the basis of machine "feeling".

## 4. Meta-RL, emergent model-based behaviour, and need-conditioned generalization

- **Wang, J. X., Kurth-Nelson, Z., Kumaran, D., Tirumala, D., Soyer, H., Leibo, J. Z., Hassabis, D., & Botvinick, M. (2018).** Prefrontal cortex as a meta-reinforcement learning system. *Nature Neuroscience*, 21(6), 860–868. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0147-8. A recurrent network trained model-free produced model-based-like behaviour, including in the two-step task. **Duan, Y., et al. (2016).** RL²: Fast reinforcement learning via slow reinforcement learning. arXiv:1611.02779. **Botvinick, M., Ritter, S., Wang, J. X., Kurth-Nelson, Z., Blundell, C., & Hassabis, D. (2019).** Reinforcement learning, fast and slow. *Trends Cogn. Sci.*, 23(5), 408–422. **[supports]** Motivates the recurrent/meta-RL PPO arm. As far as we could verify, the tasks in Wang et al. did not manipulate motivational state.
- **Lee, J., Hennig, J. A., Frelih, V., Gershman, S. J., & Uchida, N. (2025).** Emergence of rapid value inference through meta-reinforcement learning. *bioRxiv*. https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.11.30.691382. Mice and RNNs trained on repeated reversals moved from value learning that depends on plasticity to value inference carried by network dynamics, including zero-shot inference. **[supports]** Training on varied needs (our "free" need-variation arm) is the analogous manipulation.
- **Schaul, T., Horgan, D., Gregor, K., & Silver, D. (2015).** Universal value function approximators. *ICML 2015*, PMLR 37:1312–1320. **Barreto, A., Dabney, W., Munos, R., Hunt, J. J., Schaul, T., van Hasselt, H., & Silver, D. (2017).** Successor features for transfer in reinforcement learning. *NeurIPS 30*. A policy conditioned on need is a goal-conditioned value function. Successor features give zero-shot transfer to new reward weights. **[constrains]** Interpolating within trained need dimensions is expected. A never-needed nutrient tests extrapolation, which these methods do not promise.
- **Richens, J., & Everitt, T. (2024).** Robust agents learn causal world models. *ICLR 2024*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877. **Richens, J., Everitt, T., & Abel, D. (2025).** General agents need world models. *ICML 2025*, PMLR 267. **[supports]** Theory predicts that robust T2 generalization requires an (implicit) world model. A model-free agent that passes T2 should therefore contain a model that probing can extract.

## 5. Active inference: equivalences with RL, habits, and interoceptive preferences

- **Sajid, N., Ball, P. J., Parr, T., & Friston, K. J. (2021).** Active inference: Demystified and compared. *Neural Computation*, 33(3), 674–712. Without reward, active inference agents seek information, unlike Q-learning agents. Preferences can be learned. **[supports]** **[constrains]** The epistemic drive can produce responding in extinction tests that has nothing to do with outcome value.
- **Da Costa, L., Sajid, N., Parr, T., Friston, K., & Smith, R. (2023).** Reward maximization through discrete active inference. *Neural Computation*, 35(5), 807–852. https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01574. Standard active inference is Bellman-optimal only for horizon 1 on POMDPs. Sophisticated (recursive) inference is optimal for any finite horizon. **[constrains]** Planning horizon and scheme must be matched across planners, or differences in T1/T2 may simply reflect horizon.
- **Millidge, B., Tschantz, A., & Buckley, C. L. (2021).** Whence the expected free energy? *Neural Computation*, 33(2), 447–482. **Tschantz, A., Millidge, B., Seth, A. K., & Buckley, C. L. (2020).** Reinforcement learning through active inference. ICLR 2020 workshop (BAICS), arXiv:2002.12636. **Fountas, Z., Sajid, N., Mediano, P., & Friston, K. (2020).** Deep active inference agents using Monte-Carlo methods. *NeurIPS 33*. The last of these amortizes tree search into a "habitual" network. **[supports]** Deep active inference agents, including ones with a habit component, are feasible.
- **Friston, K., FitzGerald, T., Rigoli, F., Schwartenbeck, P., O'Doherty, J., & Pezzulo, G. (2016).** Active inference and learning. *Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev.*, 68, 862–879. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.06.022. Habits emerge as learned priors over policies, and the paper simulates devaluation. **[pre-empts, partly]** Devaluation in active inference has been simulated in a single tabular agent. **FitzGerald, T. H. B., Dolan, R. J., & Friston, K. J. (2014).** Model averaging, optimal inference, and habit formation. *Front. Hum. Neurosci.*, 8, 457. **Maisto, D., Friston, K., & Pezzulo, G. (2019).** Caching mechanisms for habit formation in active inference. *Neurocomputing*, 359, 298–314. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2019.05.083. **[constrains]** Whether our active inference agent can form habits at all, and so whether T3 is meaningful for it, depends on whether the policy prior (pymdp's `E`) is learned.
- **Pezzulo, G., Rigoli, F., & Friston, K. (2015).** Active inference, homeostatic regulation and adaptive behavioural control. *Progress in Neurobiology*, 134, 17–35. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2015.09.001. **Seth, A. K. (2013).** Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self. *Trends Cogn. Sci.*, 17(11), 565–573. **Seth, A. K., & Friston, K. J. (2016).** Active interoceptive inference and the emotional brain. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*, 371, 20160007. **Tschantz, A., Barca, L., Maisto, D., Buckley, C. L., Seth, A. K., & Pezzulo, G. (2022).** Simulating homeostatic, allostatic and goal-directed forms of interoceptive control using active inference. *Biological Psychology*, 169, 108266. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2022.108266. **[supports]** Preferences over interoceptive observations are the standard active-inference treatment of homeostasis. ## 8. Novelty assessment

**Already done:**
1. Devaluation and overtraining effects in computational models are well established. Most are tabular or circuit-level (Daw 2005; Keramati 2011; Mannella 2016; Miller 2019; Perez & Dickinson 2020).
2. One deep variational agent shows habitization and devaluation-insensitivity after extensive training (Han et al. 2024).
3. Active inference has simulated devaluation and habit formation (Friston et al. 2016; Maisto 2019).
4. Revaluation metrics exist for deep model-based RL (LoCA; Wan 2022), and revaluation and two-step tests exist for LLMs (Binz & Schulz 2023; CogEval).
5. Mechanisms for instant, need-weighted revaluation exist (Zhang 2009; Reward Bases 2024; homeostatic RL models of sodium appetite).
6. Deep homeostatic RL with interoception exists, including a recurrent meta-RL variant (Yoshida 2024–2025).
7. Behavioural preference and welfare tests exist for LLMs (Tagliabue & Dung 2025 and many 2025–2026 preprints).

**Appears open (nothing found):**
- A **factorial over ingredients**: interoception × need variation in training × learner class (model-free / meta-RL / learned-reward model-based / homeostatic planner / active inference), run through **one shared protocol** of devaluation in extinction, zero-shot revaluation and overtraining.
- A **Berridge-style never-needed-nutrient test applied to deep RL, meta-RL or active inference agents**. Existing instant-revaluation models either assume pre-specified reward dimensions (Reward Bases) or were not tested as agents.
- **Known-groups validation**: ground-truth random, fixed-habit and oracle agents used to validate desire assays, explicitly framed as calibration before applying indicators to LLMs for AI welfare.
- An explicit treatment of the **Pavlovian vs. instrumental / incentive-learning split** (Robinson & Berridge 2013 vs. Balleine 1992 and Roy et al. 2026) as a dimension that separates agent classes.

**Closest competitors.** Han et al. 2024 (T1/T3 in a deep agent; one architecture, no interoception). Millidge et al. 2024 (the T2 mechanism; tabular, no agent factorial). Yoshida 2025 CCN and PNAS Nexus 2024 (the interoceptive deep and meta-RL ingredients; no assays). Wan et al. 2022 (a revaluation benchmark for deep model-based RL; external reward change, not change of internal state). Friston et al. 2016 (devaluation under active inference). Tagliabue & Dung 2025 (welfare preference tests; no ground truth).

**Uncertainty.** Moderate. Search covered the major venues, arXiv and bioRxiv up to October 2026. It may have missed workshop papers, ALife/SAB proceedings, theses, non-English work, and very recent preprints. The animat and homeostatic-robotics communities (e.g., SAB) are the most likely place for an unseen overlap. The combination looks novel. Each ingredient on its own does not.

## 9. Design implications

1. **T1 protocol and index.** Use two actions, each earning a distinct outcome or nutrient. Pre-feed (satiate) one outcome. Test in **extinction**, with no outcome delivery, so the test cannot cause new learning. Counterbalance which outcome is devalued across seeds. Report an outcome-specific index, e.g. DI = (R_valued − R_devalued)/(R_valued + R_devalued), computed on early test responding and normalized to pre-devaluation baseline. This follows Colwill & Rescorla 1985a and Balleine & Dickinson 1998. Do not use raw response rate: drive can energize habits globally (Niv et al. 2006). Add a rewarded reacquisition test as a check.
2. **Manipulation check for each seed.** Confirm that satiety actually changed the relevant need variable and the agent's free-consumption choice in every seed. Exclude or separately model seeds where devaluation failed (Vázquez-Millán et al. 2026).
3. **T3 needs a habit-permissive design.** Choice between two actions with two outcomes protects goal-directed control even after extensive training (Colwill & Rescorla 1985b; Kosaki & Dickinson 2010). For T3, also run a single action plus a non-contingent second outcome, preferably on an interval-like schedule (Dickinson et al. 1983). Sample training amount on a log grid and test for a step change (Moore et al. 2026). Treat a null effect as informative, since human overtraining often fails (de Wit et al. 2018).
4. **Set T2 ground truth with incentive learning in mind.** Animals revalue *Pavlovian* cue approach instantly (Robinson & Berridge 2013). *Instrumental* performance usually needs incentive learning (Balleine 1992; Roy et al. 2026), sodium being a possible exception (Krieckhaus & Wolf 1968). So the expected T2 "pass" is the agent with **innate drive-reduction valuation over a model** (the homeostatic planner, a Berridge-type agent). A learned-scalar-reward model-based agent trained with clamped needs should fail like a Dickinson rat. Add an incentive-learning control (brief experience of the outcome in the new state) to separate "cannot revalue" from "needs incentive learning". Score cue-approach and instrumental choice separately if the environment allows.
5. **Separate interpolation from extrapolation.** Successor features, UVFAs and Reward Bases predict zero-shot revaluation only within trained need dimensions. Run T2 with the novel nutrient's need channel (a) observed but never varied and (b) absent, to separate generalization over need values from generalization over need dimensions.
6. **Guard against model-free mimicry.** Recurrent and meta-RL agents can look model-based (Akam 2015; Wang 2018; Simmons-Edler 2025). Score the first test episodes before any in-test experience of the outcome. Following Richens & Everitt, probe recurrent agents for an implicit outcome model when they pass.
7. **Match planners and control the epistemic term.** Use matched horizons, since active inference is Bellman-optimal only at horizon 1 without sophisticated inference (Da Costa 2023). Ablate the novelty/epistemic term so that exploration in extinction is not read as wanting (Sajid 2021). Decide whether pymdp's policy prior `E` is learned. If it is not, T3 is trivially null for active inference (Friston 2016; Maisto 2019).
8. **Keep habit types distinct.** The fixed-habit control is a value-free habit (Miller 2019). PPO is a cached value (Daw 2005). Report them as different habit types. Dorsolateral-striatum lesions in animals (Moore 2026) are the biological counterpart of "removing the cache" controls.
9. **Alignment and welfare framing.** Present clamped-need training as a controlled goal-misgeneralization manipulation (Langosco 2022). Present the known-groups controls as the answer to the missing-validation and gaming problems raised by Birch (2024) and Butlin et al. (2026). Note that for LLMs, the gap between stated preferences and incentives (Zhou & Ackerman 2026) is the target dissociation that devaluation-style assays would probe.
